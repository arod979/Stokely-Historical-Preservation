# Narrative Episode Framework v1.0

**Version:** 1.0  

---

# 1. Purpose

The Narrative Episode Framework defines how the Stokely Historical Preservation system organizes historical understanding into coherent, evidence-supported narratives.

Narrative episodes answer the question:

> How do we help people understand the meaning and significance of connected historical events?

Narrative episodes support:

- Autobiography development
- Museum exhibitions
- Documentary projects
- Educational experiences
- Public interpretation

---

# 2. Narrative Philosophy

## 2.1 Narratives Are Built From Evidence

A narrative episode is not created from imagination.

It is constructed from:

- Resources
- Evidence
- Historical claims
- Historical assertions
- Relationships
- Context

---

## 2.2 Narratives Preserve Meaning

A historical archive answers:

> What happened?

A narrative episode helps answer:

> Why did it matter?

---

## 2.3 Narratives Must Preserve Complexity

A good historical narrative may include:

- Success
- Failure
- Uncertainty
- Challenges
- Multiple perspectives
- Changing understanding

The goal is understanding, not creating a simplified legend.

---

# 3. Narrative Episode Structure

Each narrative episode should contain:

---

## Episode Title

A concise identifying title.

Example:

"The Rise of the Professional Disc Golf Instructor"

---

## Historical Period

The timeframe covered.

Examples:

- Early career period
- Competitive era
- Expansion period

---

## Central Question

The historical question the episode explores.

Example:

"How did competitive experience become a foundation for teaching the next generation of players?"

---

## Summary

A concise explanation of the episode.

---

## Related Entities

The people, places, organizations, events, and concepts involved.

---

## Supporting Assertions

The approved historical interpretations forming the foundation of the episode.

---

## Evidence Base

The resources supporting the episode.

---

## Narrative Context

Explanation of why the episode matters historically.

---

# 4. Narrative Episode Types

---

# 4.1 Biography Episodes

Focus on an individual's life or career.

Examples:

- Early development
- Career transformation
- Legacy

---

# 4.2 Event Episodes

Focus on significant moments.

Examples:

- Major competitions
- Milestones
- Historical turning points

---

# 4.3 Era Episodes

Focus on periods of change.

Examples:

- Growth of professional disc golf
- Development of instruction methods

---

# 4.4 Innovation Episodes

Focus on ideas, methods, or creations.

Examples:

- New teaching approaches
- Equipment development
- Cultural changes

---

# 4.5 Community Episodes

Focus on groups and shared experiences.

Examples:

- Growth of local communities
- Development of the sport culture

---

# 5. Narrative Voice Standards

Narratives may use different voices.

---

## Historical Voice

Used for institutional interpretation.

Example:

"During this period, competitive disc golf experienced significant growth."

---

## First-Person Voice

Used for personal testimony.

Example:

"I remember feeling that this was the moment everything changed."

First-person accounts must remain attributed.

---

## Combined Presentation

A narrative may combine:

- Personal experience
- Historical context
- Supporting evidence

The distinction between perspectives must remain clear.

---

# 6. Narrative Integrity Rules

## Rule 1

Narratives must not introduce unsupported events.

---

## Rule 2

Emotional impact must come from authentic historical experience.

---

## Rule 3

Personal memories should not be rewritten as objective facts.

---

## Rule 4

Important uncertainty should be preserved.

---

## Rule 5

Narratives must remain traceable to supporting evidence.

---

# 7. Narrative Development Process

A narrative episode progresses through:

---

## Stage 1 — Collection

Gather relevant resources.

---

## Stage 2 — Connection

Identify related entities and relationships.

---

## Stage 3 — Evaluation

Review claims and assertions.

---

## Stage 4 — Construction

Organize information into a coherent episode.

---

## Stage 5 — Review

Verify accuracy and rights considerations.

---

## Stage 6 — Publication

Prepare for intended audience.

---

# 8. Narrative and the Knowledge Graph

Narrative episodes are generated from connected knowledge.

Example:

Entities:

Person → Event → Organization → Place

Relationships:

Participated in → Created → Influenced

Evidence:

Resources supporting each connection

Assertions:

Approved interpretations

Narrative:

A historically grounded explanation.

---

# 9. AI and Narrative Creation

Future AI systems may assist with:

- Finding related materials
- Creating timelines
- Suggesting connections
- Drafting summaries

However:

AI must not invent:

- Events
- Emotions
- Motivations
- Relationships

Human review remains required.
