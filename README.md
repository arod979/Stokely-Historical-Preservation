# Stokely Historical Preservation

## A Museum-Grade Historical Knowledge System

The Stokely Historical Preservation repository is the private institutional foundation for preserving, organizing, verifying, and interpreting the legacy of Scott Stokely, the growth of disc golf, and related historical materials.

This repository is not simply an archive.

It is designed as a long-term historical knowledge system capable of supporting:

- Future museum exhibitions
- Public historical interpretation
- Oral history preservation
- Autobiographical projects
- Research and scholarship
- Digital preservation
- Institutional knowledge continuity

The guiding principle:

> Preserve knowledge with context, evidence, relationships, and meaning — not merely files.

---

# Mission

To create a trustworthy, permanent, and expandable historical preservation system that protects firsthand accounts, supporting evidence, and historical understanding for future generations.

The system exists to ensure that important stories, contributions, and cultural moments are not lost through time, memory limitations, technology changes, or organizational transitions.

---

# Institutional Philosophy

## Institution First. Technology Second.

The Stokely Historical Preservation system is designed to outlive:

- Individual software platforms
- Artificial intelligence systems
- Database technologies
- Storage providers
- Organizational changes

Technology serves the institution.

The institution does not serve the technology.

---

# Core Principles

## 1. Historical Integrity

The system will:

- Never create fiction or embellish history
- Clearly separate fact from interpretation
- Preserve uncertainty when information is incomplete
- Maintain evidence trails for historical claims

## 2. Firsthand Accounts Matter

Personal memories, interviews, and experiences are valuable historical resources.

However:

- Personal accounts are preserved as firsthand evidence
- They are not automatically treated as complete historical truth
- Multiple perspectives may coexist

## 3. Context Over Storage

A file without context is incomplete.

Every meaningful historical item should eventually connect to:

- Who
- What
- When
- Where
- Why
- Evidence
- Relationships
- Historical significance

## 4. Preservation Over Replacement

Original materials should be preserved.

Corrections, interpretations, and derived works should exist alongside source materials rather than replacing them.
