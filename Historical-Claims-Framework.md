# Historical Claims Framework v1.0

**Version:** 1.0  

---

# 1. Purpose

The Historical Claims Framework defines how the Stokely Historical Preservation system creates, evaluates, records, and maintains statements about historical understanding.

Historical claims answer the question:

> What can we responsibly say about the past based on available evidence?

The purpose of this framework is to ensure that historical interpretation remains:

- Evidence-based
- Transparent
- Traceable
- Open to revision

---

# 2. Historical Claims Philosophy

## 2.1 Claims Are Interpretive Structures

A claim is not simply a stored fact.

A claim is a structured historical statement supported by evidence.

Example:

Resource:

A collection of instructional videos.

Evidence:

The videos demonstrate teaching methods used by Scott Stokely.

Claim:

Scott Stokely contributed to the development and expansion of disc golf instruction.

---

## 2.2 Claims Must Remain Traceable

Every significant claim should connect to:

- Supporting resources
- Evidence evaluation
- Related entities
- Confidence level
- Interpretation notes

---

## 2.3 Claims May Evolve

Historical understanding changes as new information becomes available.

New evidence may:

- Strengthen a claim
- Refine a claim
- Complicate a claim
- Challenge a claim

Previous interpretations should not be erased without documentation.

---

# 3. Claim Structure

A historical claim consists of:

---

## Claim Statement

The actual historical assertion.

Example:

"Scott Stokely influenced the growth of disc golf instruction."

---

## Claim Type

The category of statement being made.

Examples:

- Identity claim
- Timeline claim
- Achievement claim
- Influence claim
- Cultural claim
- Causal claim

---

## Supporting Evidence

The resources and evidence that support the claim.

---

## Counter Evidence

Information that may challenge or complicate the claim.

---

## Confidence Level

The current assessment of support.

---

## Interpretation Notes

Explanation of reasoning and historical context.

---

# 4. Claim Categories

---

# 4.1 Identity Claims

Statements about who or what something is.

Examples:

- A person held a specific role.
- An organization had a specific purpose.

---

# 4.2 Timeline Claims

Statements about when events occurred.

Examples:

- A tournament occurred during a specific era.
- A career milestone occurred within a time period.

---

# 4.3 Achievement Claims

Statements about accomplishments.

Examples:

- A person won a significant event.
- An innovation changed practice.

---

# 4.4 Influence Claims

Statements about impact on people, organizations, or culture.

Examples:

- A teaching method influenced later instruction.
- A person contributed to cultural growth.

Influence claims require careful evidence.

Association alone does not prove influence.

---

# 4.5 Causal Claims

Statements explaining why something happened.

Examples:

- An innovation contributed to a change in the sport.
- A decision affected future development.

Causal claims require stronger evidence than descriptive claims.

---

# 4.6 Cultural Claims

Statements about broader meaning.

Examples:

- A movement reflected changing attitudes.
- An individual represented a larger cultural shift.

---

# 5. Claim Confidence Levels

Claims should be assigned confidence levels.

---

## Established

Strong evidence and broad agreement support the claim.

---

## Strongly Supported

Evidence is substantial, with limited uncertainty.

---

## Supported

Evidence exists but additional research may strengthen understanding.

---

## Emerging

A reasonable interpretation exists but requires further development.

---

## Unresolved

Evidence is insufficient or conflicting.

---

## Disputed

Significant disagreement or contradictory evidence exists.

---

# 6. Claim Evaluation

Claims should be evaluated using:

## Evidence Quality

How reliable are the supporting sources?

---

## Evidence Quantity

How many independent sources support the claim?

---

## Evidence Diversity

Do different types of evidence support the claim?

Examples:

- Documents
- Interviews
- Images
- Records

---

## Alternative Explanations

Could the evidence support another interpretation?

---

# 7. Claim Relationships

Claims may connect to:

## Resources

Evidence sources.

---

## Entities

Historical subjects involved.

---

## Other Claims

Supporting or related interpretations.

---

## Narratives

Larger historical explanations.

---

## Themes

Broad patterns of understanding.

---

# 8. Claim Integrity Rules

## Rule 1

Claims must not exceed available evidence.

---

## Rule 2

Interpretation must remain distinguishable from documented fact.

---

## Rule 3

Uncertainty must be visible.

---

## Rule 4

Conflicting evidence must be preserved.

---

## Rule 5

Claims should be revisable when new evidence emerges.

---

# 9. AI and Historical Claims

Future AI systems may assist with:

- Finding related evidence
- Identifying unsupported claims
- Suggesting connections
- Comparing interpretations

However:

AI systems must not independently establish historical claims.

Historical claims require human review and evidence evaluation.
