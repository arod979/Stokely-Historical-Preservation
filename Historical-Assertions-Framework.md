# Historical Assertions Framework v1.0

**Version:** 1.0  

---

# 1. Purpose

The Historical Assertions Framework defines how the Stokely Historical Preservation system identifies, evaluates, approves, and maintains established historical interpretations.

Historical assertions answer the question:

> What historical understanding is the institution prepared to present as supported interpretation?

The purpose of this framework is to prevent unsupported statements from becoming institutional history.

---

# 2. Historical Assertion Philosophy

## 2.1 Assertions Represent Evaluated Understanding

An assertion is not a raw observation.

It represents a conclusion reached through:

- Evidence review
- Historical reasoning
- Documentation
- Evaluation

---

## 2.2 Assertions Are Stronger Than Claims

The progression is:

Resource

↓

Evidence

↓

Historical Claim

↓

Historical Assertion

↓

Narrative Interpretation

---

A claim proposes an interpretation.

An assertion represents an accepted interpretation.

---

## 2.3 Assertions Remain Revisable

An accepted assertion may change when:

- New evidence appears
- Better documentation is discovered
- Historical understanding develops

Revision does not erase prior understanding.

The history of interpretation must also be preserved.

---

# 3. Assertion Structure

A historical assertion should contain:

---

## Assertion Statement

The approved historical interpretation.

Example:

"Scott Stokely played a significant role in expanding disc golf instruction through teaching, media, and educational resources."

---

## Supporting Claims

The evaluated claims that support the assertion.

---

## Evidence Base

The resources and evidence supporting those claims.

---

## Confidence Level

The institution's assessment of support.

---

## Review Status

The current institutional standing.

---

## Attribution

Who evaluated or approved the interpretation.

---

# 4. Assertion Categories

---

# 4.1 Established Historical Assertion

A well-supported interpretation with strong evidence.

Characteristics:

- Multiple supporting sources
- Clear provenance
- Minimal unresolved contradiction

---

# 4.2 Supported Historical Assertion

A credible interpretation supported by evidence but with some remaining limitations.

---

# 4.3 Emerging Historical Assertion

A developing interpretation requiring additional research.

---

# 4.4 Contested Historical Assertion

An interpretation where significant disagreement exists.

Multiple perspectives should be preserved.

---

# 4.5 Rejected or Superseded Assertion

An interpretation no longer supported by available evidence.

The historical record of the assertion should remain.

---

# 5. Assertion Approval Standards

Before becoming an institutional assertion, a statement should be evaluated for:

---

## Evidence Strength

Does sufficient evidence support the interpretation?

---

## Source Quality

Are supporting resources reliable and properly documented?

---

## Historical Context

Does the assertion consider the broader historical environment?

---

## Alternative Interpretations

Have reasonable competing explanations been considered?

---

## Transparency

Can future researchers understand how the conclusion was reached?

---

# 6. Assertion vs Personal Perspective

Personal perspectives remain valuable.

However:

A person's belief or memory should not automatically become an institutional assertion.

Example:

Personal account:

"I believe this was the turning point in my career."

Historical assertion:

"This event represented an important turning point in the athlete's career based on documented evidence and broader context."

Both are preserved.

They serve different purposes.

---

# 7. Public Interpretation Rules

Public-facing materials should distinguish:

## Documented Fact

Directly supported information.

---

## Historical Interpretation

A reasoned conclusion based on evidence.

---

## Personal Perspective

An individual's experience or belief.

---

## Unknown

Information not currently established.

---

# 8. Assertion Relationships

Assertions may connect to:

- Historical claims
- Evidence
- Resources
- Entities
- Narrative episodes
- Themes

Example:

Evidence:

Instructional videos

↓

Claims:

Demonstrated teaching methods and influence

↓

Assertion:

Contributed to the development of modern disc golf instruction

---

# 9. Assertion Integrity Rules

## Rule 1

Assertions must not exceed their evidence base.

---

## Rule 2

Institutional confidence must be visible.

---

## Rule 3

Disagreement must be preserved when historically relevant.

---

## Rule 4

Personal testimony must remain distinguished from institutional conclusions.

---

## Rule 5

Changes to assertions must be documented.

---

# 10. AI and Historical Assertions

AI systems may assist with:

- Finding evidence
- Comparing sources
- Identifying gaps
- Suggesting possible interpretations

AI systems may not independently establish historical assertions.

Final approval requires human historical judgment.
