# Evidence Framework v1.0

**Version:** 1.0  

---

# 1. Purpose

The Evidence Framework defines how the Stokely Historical Preservation system evaluates, records, and uses evidence when developing historical understanding.

Evidence management answers the question:

> What supports this historical understanding, and how strongly?

The goal is not to determine history through a single source.

The goal is to create a transparent chain between:

Resource → Evidence → Claim → Narrative → Public Understanding

---

# 2. Evidence Philosophy

## 2.1 Resources and Evidence Are Different

A resource is something preserved.

Evidence is the role that a resource plays in supporting historical understanding.

Example:

Resource:

A recorded interview with Scott Stokely.

Evidence:

A firsthand account supporting information about personal experiences, decisions, and perspectives.

---

## 2.2 Evidence Does Not Equal Absolute Truth

Evidence supports understanding.

It does not eliminate the need for:

- Context
- Comparison
- Verification
- Interpretation

---

## 2.3 Multiple Evidence Types Matter

Different forms of evidence provide different kinds of knowledge.

A firsthand account may reveal personal experience.

A contemporary document may establish timing.

A photograph may confirm presence.

A statistical record may verify achievement.

Each contributes differently.

---

# 3. Evidence Categories

The system recognizes the following evidence types.

---

# 3.1 Primary Evidence

## Definition

Evidence created during the historical period by participants or contemporaries.

Examples:

- Original documents
- Photographs
- Videos
- Event records
- Personal correspondence

Primary evidence provides direct connection to historical moments.

---

# 3.2 Firsthand Accounts

## Definition

Personal testimony from someone who experienced an event or period.

Examples:

- Interviews
- Oral histories
- Personal recollections

Firsthand accounts preserve unique knowledge.

They should be identified as personal perspectives, not automatically treated as complete historical records.

---

# 3.3 Contemporary Records

## Definition

Records created close to the time of an event.

Examples:

- Tournament results
- Publications
- News coverage
- Official records

---

# 3.4 Secondary Sources

## Definition

Later interpretations or analyses created using historical materials.

Examples:

- Books
- Articles
- Documentaries
- Research summaries

---

# 3.5 Community Memory

## Definition

Shared knowledge within a community.

Examples:

- Stories passed through participants
- Cultural understanding
- Collective recollections

Community memory is valuable but requires appropriate documentation.

---

# 4. Evidence Evaluation

Evidence should be evaluated according to:

---

## 4.1 Authenticity

Question:

> Is this what it claims to be?

Consider:

- Source verification
- Originality
- Provenance

---

## 4.2 Reliability

Question:

> How dependable is this evidence for this specific purpose?

A source may be reliable for one question but not another.

---

## 4.3 Proximity

Question:

> How close is the source to the event?

Examples:

- Direct participant account
- Contemporary record
- Later recollection

---

## 4.4 Corroboration

Question:

> Do other sources support or challenge this information?

Multiple independent sources increase confidence.

---

## 4.5 Bias and Perspective

Every source has context.

Consider:

- Creator perspective
- Purpose
- Audience
- Limitations

Bias does not make evidence unusable.

It helps explain how evidence should be interpreted.

---

# 5. Evidence Confidence Levels

Evidence may be classified as:

## Strong

Well-supported by reliable sources.

---

## Moderate

Supported but with limitations or incomplete information.

---

## Weak

Possible support exists but requires further verification.

---

## Unknown

Insufficient information available.

---

# 6. Evidence Chains

Historical understanding should maintain traceability.

Example:

Resource:

1995 tournament video

↓

Evidence:

Shows participation and event conditions

↓

Claim:

The event represents an important stage in competitive disc golf development

↓

Narrative:

Growth of professional disc golf

---

# 7. Oral History Standards

Oral histories receive special treatment.

They preserve:

- Memory
- Experience
- Perspective
- Motivation
- Cultural context

However:

Memory may contain:

- Approximate dates
- Personal interpretation
- Unintentional errors

The system preserves both:

- What the person remembers
- What broader evidence indicates

Neither should erase the other.

---

# 8. Evidence and AI Systems

Future AI systems may assist with:

- Finding supporting resources
- Identifying contradictions
- Suggesting connections
- Organizing evidence chains

However:

AI-generated conclusions are not evidence.

AI outputs require human review and supporting sources.

---

# 9. Evidence Integrity Rules

## Rule 1

Evidence must remain connected to its source.

---

## Rule 2

Personal accounts must remain identified as personal accounts.

---

## Rule 3

Uncertainty must be preserved.

---

## Rule 4

Contradictory evidence must be documented, not hidden.

---

## Rule 5

Interpretation must remain distinguishable from evidence.

---
