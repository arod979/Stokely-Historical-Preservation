# Relationship Model v1.0

**Version:** 1.0

---

# 1. Purpose

The Relationship Model defines how historical entities connect within the Stokely Historical Preservation system.

The entity model defines:

> What exists.

The relationship model defines:

> How historical elements connect and influence one another.

History is not a collection of isolated facts.

History is a network of:

- People
- Events
- Places
- Organizations
- Resources
- Ideas
- Influences

---

# 2. Relationship Philosophy

## 2.1 Relationships Carry Meaning

A relationship is not simply a connection.

A meaningful historical relationship explains:

- What connects two entities
- Why the connection matters
- What evidence supports it
- How certain the relationship is

---

## 2.2 Relationships Require Context

Example:

Weak relationship:

Scott Stokely → Oak Grove Park

Strong relationship:

Scott Stokely participated in the early development of disc golf culture connected to Oak Grove Park through competition, experience, and historical significance.

The second preserves meaning.

---

## 2.3 Relationships May Change

Historical understanding evolves.

A relationship may become:

- Better documented
- More precise
- Reinterpreted
- Disputed

The system preserves the evolution of understanding.

---

# 3. Relationship Structure

A relationship consists of:

## Subject

The entity initiating the relationship.

Example:

Scott Stokely

---

## Relationship Type

The nature of the connection.

Example:

Participated In

---

## Object

The entity connected to the subject.

Example:

Tournament Event

---

## Evidence

The resources supporting the relationship.

Example:

Tournament record, interview, photograph

---

## Confidence

The current reliability of the relationship.

Examples:

- Confirmed
- Probable
- Possible
- Unknown

---

# 4. Core Relationship Categories

---

# 4.1 Participation Relationships

## Definition

Connects entities involved in historical activities or events.

## Examples

- Person participated in Event
- Organization participated in Event

---

# 4.2 Creation Relationships

## Definition

Connects creators to things they produced.

## Examples

- Person created Resource
- Organization created Publication
- Person developed Concept

---

# 4.3 Ownership Relationships

## Definition

Documents possession or control.

## Examples

- Person owns Artifact
- Organization owns Collection

Ownership must include dates when possible.

---

# 4.4 Influence Relationships

## Definition

Documents historical impact.

## Examples

- Person influenced Person
- Concept influenced Activity
- Organization influenced Movement

Influence relationships require careful evidence.

Influence should not be assumed simply because two entities are connected.

---

# 4.5 Location Relationships

## Definition

Connects entities to places.

## Examples

- Event occurred at Place
- Person associated with Place
- Organization located at Place

---

# 4.6 Temporal Relationships

## Definition

Documents historical sequence and timing.

## Examples

- Event followed Event
- Activity occurred during Time Period
- Organization existed during Era

---

# 4.7 Documentation Relationships

## Definition

Connects resources to historical subjects.

## Examples

- Resource documents Event
- Interview documents Person
- Photograph depicts Event

---

# 4.8 Evidence Relationships

## Definition

Connects evidence to historical claims.

## Examples

- Resource supports Claim
- Interview provides evidence for Assertion

---

# 5. Relationship Direction

Relationships should preserve direction when meaningful.

Example:

Person → created → Resource

is different from:

Resource → depicts → Person

The direction provides historical meaning.

---

# 6. Relationship Confidence

Relationships should include confidence levels.

## Confirmed

Strong supporting evidence exists.

---

## Probable

Evidence strongly suggests the relationship but some uncertainty remains.

---

## Possible

A reasonable connection exists but requires further verification.

---

## Unknown

The relationship is suspected but unsupported.

---

# 7. Relationship Evidence Requirements

Important relationships should identify supporting sources.

Examples:

Claim:

Scott Stokely influenced disc golf instruction.

Supporting relationships:

- Created instructional materials
- Conducted seminars
- Taught students
- Produced educational content

Each connection requires evidence.

---

# 8. Relationship Integrity Rules

## Rule 1

A relationship must not exist solely because it is convenient.

---

## Rule 2

Relationships must distinguish association from causation.

Example:

Being present at an event does not automatically mean causing an outcome.

---

## Rule 3

Influence claims require stronger evidence than simple participation.

---

## Rule 4

Unknown relationships remain unknown.

---

# 9. Knowledge Graph Foundation

Relationships are the foundation of the future knowledge graph.

The knowledge graph will allow the institution to understand:

- Who influenced whom
- Which events shaped history
- Which resources support which claims
- How themes developed over time
