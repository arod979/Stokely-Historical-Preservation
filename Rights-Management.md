# Rights Management Framework v1.0

**Version:** 1.0

---

# 1. Purpose

The Rights Management Framework defines how the Stokely Historical Preservation system records, protects, and manages legal and ethical usage considerations associated with preserved materials.

Rights management answers the question:

> What can we responsibly do with this resource?

The goal is to preserve history while respecting:

- Creators
- Owners
- Subjects
- Contributors
- Legal rights
- Ethical considerations

---

# 2. Rights Management Philosophy

## 2.1 Preservation Does Not Equal Ownership

Possessing or preserving a resource does not automatically grant rights to:

- Reproduce
- Publish
- Modify
- Sell
- Exhibit
- Distribute

Ownership, custody, and usage rights are separate concepts.

---

## 2.2 Historical Importance Does Not Remove Restrictions

A historically significant resource may still have:

- Copyright restrictions
- Privacy considerations
- Licensing limitations
- Permission requirements

Historical value and legal rights must both be preserved.

---

## 2.3 Rights Are Part of Historical Context

Future researchers need to understand:

- Who controls the resource
- How it may be used
- What permissions were granted
- What restrictions remain

Rights information is part of the resource history.

---

# 3. Rights Categories

The system recognizes the following rights categories.

---

# 3.1 Copyright

## Definition

Legal protection granted to creators of original works.

Examples:

- Photographs
- Videos
- Written materials
- Publications
- Artwork

Metadata should record:

- Copyright holder
- Copyright status
- Known expiration information
- Permission requirements

---

# 3.2 Ownership Rights

## Definition

Legal ownership of a physical or digital item.

Ownership does not necessarily include copyright.

Example:

A person may own an original photograph print while another party owns the copyright.

---

# 3.3 Usage Rights

## Definition

Permissions allowing specific activities.

Examples:

- Internal research
- Educational use
- Exhibition display
- Publication
- Digital distribution

---

# 3.4 Licensing Rights

## Definition

Permissions granted through an agreement.

Examples:

- Limited exhibition license
- Media license
- Educational license
- Digital archive license

Licenses should document:

- Scope
- Duration
- Restrictions
- Granting party

---

# 3.5 Privacy and Personal Rights

## Definition

Rights relating to individuals represented in resources.

Considerations include:

- Personal privacy
- Publicity rights
- Consent
- Sensitive information

---

# 4. Rights Metadata Requirements

Every resource should attempt to record:

## Rights Holder

Who controls applicable rights.

---

## Ownership Status

Examples:

- Owned by institution
- Privately owned
- Loaned
- Unknown

---

## Copyright Status

Examples:

- Known copyright holder
- Public domain
- Permission required
- Unknown

---

## Usage Permissions

Examples:

- Internal preservation only
- Research use
- Educational use
- Public exhibition
- Publication permitted

---

## Restrictions

Known limitations.

Examples:

- No commercial use
- Attribution required
- Permission required before publication

---

# 5. Rights States

Resources may have different rights states.

---

## Cleared

Rights are understood and approved for intended use.

---

## Partially Cleared

Some uses are permitted while others require additional permission.

---

## Restricted

Use is limited.

---

## Unknown

Rights status has not been established.

Unknown rights must not be treated as unrestricted.

---

# 6. Rights Acquisition Process

When acquiring materials, the institution should attempt to document:

## Contributor Agreement

Who provided the material and under what terms.

---

## Rights Transfer

Whether ownership or permissions were transferred.

---

## Intended Use

Why the material is being collected.

Examples:

- Preservation
- Research
- Exhibition
- Publication

---

## Future Use Considerations

Potential future applications should be considered when possible.

---

# 7. Third-Party Materials

External materials may still be preserved as references.

Examples:

- News articles
- Videos
- Websites
- Social media posts

The system should preserve:

- Source information
- Access information
- Rights status
- Usage limitations

---

# 8. Rights and Oral Histories

Oral histories require special attention.

Considerations include:

- Interviewee consent
- Recording rights
- Publication permissions
- Future exhibition use

A recorded conversation may require separate permissions for:

- Preservation
- Transcript creation
- Public release
- Commercial use

---

# 9. Rights and AI Systems

Future AI systems may assist with:

- Rights classification
- Missing permission detection
- Duplicate identification
- Usage tracking

However:

AI cannot determine legal ownership.

AI-generated rights recommendations require human review.

---

# 10. Rights Integrity Rules

## Rule 1

Unknown rights remain unknown.

---

## Rule 2

Preservation access does not imply publication rights.

---

## Rule 3

Creators and rights holders should receive appropriate attribution.

---

## Rule 4

Rights records must remain traceable.

---

## Rule 5

Historical preservation and ethical stewardship must be balanced.

---

# 11. Future Expansion

The Rights Management Framework should support:

- Museum exhibitions
- Books and publications
- Educational programs
- Digital archives
- Licensing partnerships
- Community collections

---

# 12. Status

Version 1.0 establishes the foundational rights management framework.
