# Provenance Standards v1.0

**Version:** 1.0

---

# 1. Purpose

The Provenance Standards define how the Stokely Historical Preservation system records the origin, ownership, custody, modification history, and reliability of historical resources.

Provenance answers the foundational question:

> Where did this information come from, and how did it arrive here?

A historical institution must preserve not only materials, but also the story of those materials.

---

# 2. Provenance Philosophy

## 2.1 Origin Matters

Every resource has a history before entering the preservation system.

Understanding that history provides context.

Example:

A photograph may have been:

- Taken by a tournament photographer
- Published in a magazine
- Saved by a fan
- Donated by a collector
- Digitized for preservation

Each step contributes to understanding the resource.

---

## 2.2 Provenance Is Broader Than Ownership

Ownership is only one part of provenance.

A resource may involve:

- Creator
- Subject
- Owner
- Custodian
- Contributor
- Rights holder
- Preservation institution

These roles must not be confused.

---

## 2.3 Provenance Preserves Trust

Historical conclusions are stronger when researchers can trace:

- The original source
- The path of transmission
- Any changes made
- Supporting documentation

---

# 3. Provenance Components

Every significant resource should attempt to capture the following provenance information.

---

# 3.1 Creation Information

Documents the origin of the resource.

Possible fields:

- Creator
- Creation date
- Creation location
- Original format
- Circumstances of creation

---

# 3.2 Acquisition Information

Documents how the resource entered the collection.

Possible fields:

- Acquisition date
- Source of acquisition
- Acquisition method
- Contributor
- Related agreement or permission

Examples:

- Donated by individual
- Licensed from organization
- Digitized from physical item
- Collected from public source

---

# 3.3 Ownership History

Documents known ownership changes.

Possible fields:

- Previous owners
- Ownership dates
- Transfer method
- Current owner

---

# 3.4 Custody History

Documents who maintained possession or responsibility.

Ownership and custody may differ.

Example:

A museum may hold an artifact while another party retains ownership.

---

# 3.5 Modification History

Documents changes made after creation.

Examples:

- Digitization
- Restoration
- Compression
- Editing
- Transcription
- Translation
- Format conversion

Original versions should be preserved whenever possible.

---

# 4. Provenance Roles

The system recognizes the following roles.

---

## Creator

The person or organization that created the resource.

Example:

Photographer who captured an image.

---

## Subject

The person, place, event, or concept represented by the resource.

Example:

Athlete shown in a photograph.

---

## Contributor

The person or organization that provides the resource to the institution.

---

## Owner

The party with legal ownership rights.

---

## Custodian

The party responsible for maintaining the resource.

---

## Rights Holder

The party controlling usage permissions.

---

# 5. Provenance Records

A provenance record should include:

## Resource Identifier

The item being documented.

---

## Provenance Events

A timeline of important changes.

Examples:

- Created
- Acquired
- Transferred
- Digitized
- Modified
- Published

---

## Supporting Documentation

Evidence supporting provenance claims.

Examples:

- Receipts
- Emails
- Agreements
- Interviews
- Catalog records

---

# 6. Provenance Confidence

Not all provenance information has equal certainty.

Confidence levels:

## Confirmed

Supported by documentation or reliable evidence.

---

## Probable

Strong indication exists, but documentation is incomplete.

---

## Possible

Reasonable possibility requiring further research.

---

## Unknown

Information has not been established.

---

# 7. Provenance and External Sources

External materials may be valuable even when ownership is unavailable.

Examples:

- Online articles
- Social media posts
- Archived websites
- Published interviews

The system should preserve:

- Source location
- Access date
- Available attribution
- Preservation notes

---

# 8. Provenance and Historical Claims

Provenance strengthens historical claims by showing:

- Where evidence originated
- How reliable the source may be
- Whether the material has been altered

Example:

Claim:

Scott Stokely influenced disc golf instruction.

Supporting provenance:

- Original instructional materials
- Recorded seminars
- Student accounts
- Published references

---

# 9. AI and Provenance

Future AI systems may assist with:

- Duplicate detection
- Attribution suggestions
- Missing provenance identification
- Relationship discovery

However:

AI-generated provenance suggestions are not accepted as historical records without review.

---

# 10. Provenance Integrity Rules

## Rule 1

Unknown provenance must remain unknown.

---

## Rule 2

Assumptions must not be recorded as facts.

---

## Rule 3

Original provenance information must not be replaced by later interpretations.

---

## Rule 4

Changes to provenance records must be traceable.

---

# 11. Future Expansion

The provenance framework should support:

- Museum acquisitions
- Private collections
- Oral histories
- Community contributions
- Research partnerships
- International collections

---

# 12. Status

Version 1.0 establishes the foundational provenance standards.
