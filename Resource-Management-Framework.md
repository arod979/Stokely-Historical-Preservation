# Resource Management Framework v1.0

**Version:** 1.0

---

# 1. Purpose

The Resource Management Framework defines how historical materials enter, exist within, and move through the Stokely Historical Preservation system.

Resources are the foundational materials from which historical understanding is built.

A resource may be:

- Evidence
- Context
- A record of experience
- A representation of a historical moment

However:

A resource is not automatically historical truth.

Resources require context, provenance, and interpretation.

---

# 2. Resource Philosophy

## 2.1 Preserve First, Interpret Second

The first responsibility of the institution is preservation.

Interpretation comes after preservation.

A resource should be protected before conclusions are drawn about its meaning.

---

## 2.2 Original Materials Have Priority

Whenever possible:

- Preserve original files.
- Maintain original formats.
- Record transformations.
- Preserve derivative versions separately.

A restored photograph should not replace the original photograph.

A transcript should not replace the original recording.

---

## 2.3 Resources Are Contextual

A resource gains meaning through relationships.

A photograph may become historically significant because it connects to:

- A person
- An event
- A location
- A career milestone
- A cultural change

---

# 3. Resource Categories

The system recognizes the following primary resource classes.

---

# 3.1 Digital Media

Examples:

- Photographs
- Videos
- Audio recordings
- Digital artwork
- Social media content

---

# 3.2 Documents

Examples:

- Articles
- Books
- Publications
- Letters
- Notes
- Records
- Presentations

---

# 3.3 Oral Histories

Examples:

- Interviews
- Personal recollections
- Recorded conversations
- Testimonies

Oral histories receive special preservation consideration because they capture knowledge that may not exist elsewhere.

---

# 3.4 Physical Artifacts

Examples:

- Discs
- Equipment
- Awards
- Memorabilia
- Personal items

Physical artifacts require additional considerations:

- Condition
- Location
- Ownership
- Storage requirements

---

# 3.5 External References

Examples:

- Websites
- Online articles
- Third-party archives
- Media appearances

External references should be documented even when the original material is not owned by the institution.

---

# 4. Resource Lifecycle

Every resource follows a preservation lifecycle.

---

# Stage 1 — Discovery

## Purpose

Identify potential historical materials.

Sources may include:

- Personal collections
- Interviews
- Websites
- Social media
- Archives
- Community contributions

At discovery, limited information is acceptable.

The goal is not immediate completeness.

The goal is preventing loss.

---

# Stage 2 — Acquisition

## Purpose

Bring the resource under preservation management.

Possible acquisition methods:

- Donation
- Loan
- Purchase
- Digital capture
- Permission-based collection
- External reference recording

Acquisition must document:

- Source
- Date acquired
- Ownership status
- Rights considerations

---

# Stage 3 — Registration

## Purpose

Create the initial institutional record.

Minimum requirements:

- Identifier
- Title
- Resource type
- Source
- Basic description

The resource officially enters the system.

---

# Stage 4 — Preservation

## Purpose

Protect the resource from loss or degradation.

Actions may include:

- Secure storage
- Backup
- Format preservation
- Digitization
- Integrity checks

---

# Stage 5 — Enrichment

## Purpose

Add historical context.

Possible additions:

- People identified
- Dates confirmed
- Locations identified
- Related events connected
- Additional sources linked

---

# Stage 6 — Verification

## Purpose

Assess historical reliability.

Questions include:

- Is the source known?
- Is the date accurate?
- Are people correctly identified?
- Does supporting evidence exist?

---

# Stage 7 — Interpretation

## Purpose

Allow resources to contribute to:

- Historical claims
- Narrative episodes
- Exhibitions
- Educational materials

Interpretation must remain connected to evidence.

---

# 5. Resource States

A resource may exist in different states.

## Discovered

Potential resource identified.

---

## Registered

Resource has entered the system with minimum metadata.

---

## Preserved

Resource has appropriate protection.

---

## Enriched

Additional context has been added.

---

## Verified

Important details have supporting evidence.

---

## Interpreted

Resource contributes to historical understanding.

---

# 6. Resource Quality Standards

## Authenticity

The resource should maintain a known origin whenever possible.

---

## Integrity

The resource should remain unchanged unless modifications are documented.

---

## Context

The resource should connect to relevant entities and events.

---

## Accessibility

Authorized users should be able to locate and understand the resource.

---

# 7. Resource Relationship to Evidence

A resource may:

- Contain evidence
- Support evidence
- Provide context
- Challenge existing assumptions

The system must distinguish:

Resource:

"The interview recording."

Evidence:

"The statements within the interview used to support a historical claim."

---

# 8. Resource Relationship to Claims

Resources may support historical claims.

Example:

Resource:
A 1995 tournament video.

Evidence:
Shows participation and event conditions.

Claim:
This event contributed to the growth of competitive disc golf.

The claim requires interpretation beyond the resource itself.

---
