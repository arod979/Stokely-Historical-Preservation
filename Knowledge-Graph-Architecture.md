# Knowledge Graph Architecture v1.0

**Version:** 1.0 

---

# 1. Purpose

The Knowledge Graph Architecture defines how the Stokely Historical Preservation system represents interconnected historical understanding.

The knowledge graph connects:

- Entities
- Resources
- Relationships
- Evidence
- Historical claims
- Narrative episodes
- Themes

into a structured model of historical knowledge.

---

# 2. Knowledge Graph Philosophy

## 2.1 The Graph Represents Meaning

A collection of resources tells us what materials exist.

A knowledge graph helps explain:

- How materials connect
- Why they matter
- What they support
- How historical understanding develops

---

## 2.2 Evidence-Aware Knowledge

A traditional knowledge graph may represent:

Person → Event

A historical knowledge graph represents:

Person → participated in → Event

Supported by:

Resource → Evidence

With:

Confidence Level

This preserves historical discipline.

---

## 2.3 The Graph Does Not Replace Research

The knowledge graph organizes understanding.

It does not create historical truth automatically.

All conclusions remain dependent on:

- Evidence
- Provenance
- Verification
- Interpretation

---

# 3. Graph Components

The knowledge graph consists of five primary components:

---

# 3.1 Nodes

Nodes represent things that exist within the historical system.

Primary node types:

## Entity Nodes

Examples:

- Person
- Organization
- Place
- Event
- Resource
- Artifact
- Concept

---

## Evidence Nodes

Represent materials used to support understanding.

Examples:

- Interview
- Document
- Photograph
- Video
- Record

---

## Claim Nodes

Represent documented historical statements.

Example:

"Scott Stokely contributed significantly to disc golf instruction."

---

## Narrative Nodes

Represent structured historical stories.

Example:

"The development of modern disc golf instruction."

---

## Theme Nodes

Represent broad historical patterns.

Example:

"Growth and professionalization of disc golf."

---

# 4. Edges

Edges represent relationships between nodes.

Examples:

Person
→ participated in
Event

Resource
→ documents
Event

Evidence
→ supports
Claim

Claim
→ contributes to
Narrative

Narrative
→ illustrates
Theme

---

# 5. Knowledge Graph Layers

The graph follows the institutional architecture.

---

## Layer 1 — Resource Layer

Contains preserved materials.

Examples:

- Photos
- Videos
- Interviews
- Documents

---

## Layer 2 — Entity Layer

Identifies historical subjects.

Examples:

- People
- Places
- Organizations
- Events

---

## Layer 3 — Relationship Layer

Defines connections.

Examples:

- Participated in
- Created
- Influenced
- Located at

---

## Layer 4 — Evidence Layer

Explains why relationships and claims are supported.

---

## Layer 5 — Interpretation Layer

Builds:

- Historical claims
- Narratives
- Themes
- Public interpretation

---

# 6. Graph Questions

The knowledge graph should support historical research questions.

Examples:

## Career Questions

What events shaped Scott Stokely's career?

---

## Influence Questions

Who influenced the development of disc golf instruction?

---

## Evidence Questions

What resources support this historical claim?

---

## Museum Questions

Which stories have sufficient evidence for public exhibition?

---

# 7. Confidence and Uncertainty

Knowledge graph relationships should include confidence.

Possible values:

- Confirmed
- Probable
- Possible
- Unknown
- Disputed

Uncertainty is preserved rather than hidden.

---

# 8. Historical Claims in the Graph

Claims are not treated as facts simply because they exist.

Each claim should connect to:

- Supporting resources
- Evidence
- Related entities
- Confidence level
- Interpretation notes

---

# 9. AI Integration Principles

Future AI systems may assist with:

- Identifying possible relationships
- Finding missing connections
- Suggesting research paths
- Generating timelines
- Preparing narratives

However:

AI suggestions are not historical conclusions.

All AI-generated relationships require human review and evidence evaluation.

---

# 10. Graph Evolution

The knowledge graph will expand over time.

New information may:

- Add relationships
- Strengthen evidence
- Revise interpretations
- Introduce new perspectives

Existing historical records should not be erased simply because understanding evolves.

---

# 11. Future Applications

The knowledge graph may support:

- Museum exhibitions
- Interactive timelines
- Research tools
- Educational experiences
- Autobiographical projects
- Digital historians

---
