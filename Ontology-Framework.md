# Ontology Framework v1.0

---

# 1. Purpose

The Ontology Framework defines the conceptual structure of the Stokely Historical Preservation system.

Its purpose is to answer the foundational question:

> What exists within the historical universe we are preserving?

The ontology establishes the categories, definitions, and relationships necessary to preserve history as an interconnected system of knowledge rather than as a collection of isolated files.

The ontology is intentionally independent of any specific:

- Database
- Software platform
- Artificial intelligence system
- Storage method

Technology may change.

The conceptual model must endure.

---

# 2. Ontology Principles

## 2.1 Reality Before Representation

The ontology represents historical reality.

It does not begin with:

- File formats
- Database structures
- Software limitations
- Search requirements

Those are implementation concerns.

---

## 2.2 Entities Are More Than Records

A historical subject is not merely a data entry.

A person is not only:

- Name
- Birth date
- Career statistics

A person may include:

- Experiences
- Roles
- Relationships
- Contributions
- Influence
- Historical significance

---

## 2.3 Multiple Perspectives Are Preserved

Historical entities may have:

- Different interpretations
- Conflicting accounts
- Changing understanding over time

The ontology preserves these relationships rather than forcing artificial certainty.

---

## 2.4 Context Is Required

Entities should not exist in isolation.

Meaning comes from connections:

- Who was involved?
- What happened?
- Where did it occur?
- When did it occur?
- Why did it matter?

---

# 3. Foundational Ontology Classes

The Stokely Historical Preservation system recognizes the following primary classes.

---

# 3.1 Person

## Definition

A human individual relevant to historical preservation.

A person may have multiple roles throughout their life.

## Examples

- Scott Stokely
- Disc golf pioneers
- Competitors
- Instructors
- Event organizers
- Media creators
- Community members

## Possible Roles

- Athlete
- Instructor
- Author
- Innovator
- Entrepreneur
- Organizer
- Historian
- Collector
- Advocate

A person's identity must not be reduced to a single role.

---

# 3.2 Organization

## Definition

A structured group, company, association, institution, or collective.

## Examples

- Disc golf organizations
- Manufacturers
- Clubs
- Media organizations
- Historical institutions

## Possible Types

- Sports organization
- Business
- Nonprofit
- Educational institution
- Media organization
- Community group

---

# 3.3 Place

## Definition

A geographic location with historical significance.

## Examples

- Oak Grove Park
- Tournament locations
- Disc golf courses
- Training locations
- Museums
- Exhibition spaces

## Importance

Places provide historical context.

A location is not only a coordinate.

It may represent:

- Origin
- Transformation
- Achievement
- Cultural significance

---

# 3.4 Event

## Definition

A significant occurrence bounded by time and location.

## Examples

- Tournament
- Interview
- Exhibition
- Publication release
- Career milestone
- Historical moment

## Event Attributes

Possible attributes include:

- Date
- Location
- Participants
- Organizations involved
- Related resources
- Historical impact

---

# 3.5 Time Period

## Definition

A defined period used to organize historical understanding.

## Examples

- Early disc golf era
- Professional touring era
- Modern instructional era

Time periods provide historical structure beyond individual dates.

---

# 3.6 Resource

## Definition

Any preserved item containing historical value.

## Examples

- Photograph
- Video
- Audio recording
- Interview
- Document
- Article
- Website
- Social media post
- Artifact

A resource is evidence-bearing material.

A resource is not automatically historical truth.

---

# 3.7 Artifact

## Definition

A physical object preserved for historical significance.

## Examples

- Discs
- Equipment
- Awards
- Memorabilia
- Personal items

Artifacts may require:

- Ownership records
- Condition records
- Provenance tracking

---

# 3.8 Concept

## Definition

An abstract idea or principle relevant to historical understanding.

## Examples

- Disc golf culture
- Teaching philosophy
- Athletic innovation
- Competitive strategy

Concepts allow preservation of ideas, not only objects.

---

# 3.9 Achievement

## Definition

A recognized accomplishment or contribution.

## Examples

- Tournament victory
- Career milestone
- Innovation
- Publication
- Influence on the sport

Achievements must connect to supporting evidence.

---

# 3.10 Activity

## Definition

A recurring practice, behavior, or process.

## Examples

- Touring
- Teaching
- Training
- Media creation
- Course development

Activities help explain how history unfolded.

---

# 3.11 Narrative Episode

## Definition

A structured historical story built from connected entities, resources, evidence, and interpretation.

## Examples

- The rise of professional disc golf instruction
- The development of a competitive career
- The growth of disc golf culture

Narrative episodes must originate from evidence.

---

# 3.12 Theme

## Definition

A broad historical pattern connecting multiple entities and events.

## Examples

- Growth of disc golf
- Evolution of professional athletes
- Expansion of instructional methods

Themes represent higher-level understanding.

---

# 4. Entity Hierarchy

The ontology follows this general hierarchy:

    Historical Universe

        Entities

            Person
            Organization
            Place
            Event
            Time Period
            Resource
            Artifact
            Concept
            Achievement
            Activity

        Interpretive Structures

            Narrative Episode

---

# 5. Ontology Relationships

Entities gain meaning through relationships.

Core relationship types include:

---

## Person Relationships

Examples:

- Person participated in Event
- Person created Resource
- Person influenced Person
- Person founded Organization
- Person taught Person

---

## Event Relationships

Examples:

- Event occurred at Place
- Event involved Person
- Event hosted by Organization
- Event documented by Resource

---

## Resource Relationships

Examples:

- Resource created by Person
- Resource documents Event
- Resource supports Claim
- Resource contains Evidence

---

## Historical Understanding Relationships

Examples:

- Evidence supports Claim
- Claim contributes to Narrative Episode
- Narrative Episode illustrates Theme

---

# 6. Historical Integrity Rules

## Rule 1

No entity exists without context.

---

## Rule 2

No claim exists without evidence.

---

## Rule 3

No interpretation replaces the underlying evidence.

---

## Rule 4

Unknown information remains unknown.

---

## Rule 5

Historical entities may have evolving understanding.

New evidence may update interpretation without deleting historical records.

---

# 7. Extensibility

The ontology is designed to support future collections.

Future additions may include:

- Additional athletes
- Other historical figures
- Related sports history
- Institutional collections
- Museum acquisitions

Expansion must preserve the original principles.

---

Version 1.0 establishes the foundational conceptual model.

Future revisions must maintain compatibility with:

- Constitution principles
- Glossary definitions
- Historical integrity standards
