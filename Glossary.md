# Historical Preservation Glossary v1.0

## Archive

A structured collection of preserved historical materials.

An archive contains resources but may not contain the relationships and interpretations required for deeper understanding.

---

## Assertion

A statement about history that requires support and context.

Example:

"Scott Stokely influenced disc golf instruction."

An assertion requires evidence and interpretation.

---

## Claim

A documented historical statement supported by evidence.

Claims should identify:

- Supporting resources
- Confidence level
- Interpretation

---

## Entity

A distinct historical subject.

Examples:

- Person
- Organization
- Location
- Event
- Object

---

## Evidence

Material that supports historical understanding.

Examples:

- Interviews
- Documents
- Media
- Records
- Artifacts

---

## Knowledge Graph

A connected representation of historical entities and their relationships.

A knowledge graph explains how historical elements connect.

---

## Metadata

Information describing a resource.

Examples:

- Date
- Creator
- Location
- Format
- Rights
- Source

---

## Narrative Episode

A structured historical story built from connected evidence and events.

Episodes preserve meaningful moments while maintaining historical accuracy.

---

## Ontology

The conceptual structure defining what types of things exist in the system and how they relate.

---

## Provenance

The documented history of a resource.

Answers:

- Where did it come from?
- Who created it?
- How has it changed?

---

## Resource

Any item preserved for historical value.

Examples:

- Photo
- Video
- Interview
- Document
- Website
- Artifact

---

## Relationship

A documented connection between entities.

Examples:

- Person participated in event.
- Organization created resource.
- Location hosted event.

---

## Theme

A broader historical pattern connecting multiple events, people, and resources.

Examples:

- Growth of disc golf
- Evolution of professional instruction
- Cultural impact of athletes

---

## Public Interpretation

The process of presenting historical knowledge to audiences.

Examples:

- Museum exhibits
- Articles
- Educational materials

---

# Foundation Statement

The Stokely Historical Preservation system exists to ensure that history is not merely remembered, but responsibly preserved, understood, and passed forward.

Preserve the evidence.  
Protect the context.  
Honor the story.
