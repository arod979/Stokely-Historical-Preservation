# Metadata Standards v1.0

**Version:** 1.0

---

# 1. Purpose

The Metadata Standards define the information required to identify, describe, organize, connect, preserve, and interpret historical resources and entities within the Stokely Historical Preservation system.

The ontology defines:

> What exists.

Metadata defines:

> What we know about what exists.

Metadata provides the structure that transforms collected materials into a meaningful historical knowledge system.

---

# 2. Metadata Philosophy

## 2.1 Metadata Creates Context

A resource without metadata is only an object.

A photograph without context is simply an image.

A photograph with:

- Creator
- Date
- Location
- People involved
- Event connection
- Historical significance
- Provenance

becomes a historical resource.

---

## 2.2 Progressive Enrichment

Metadata should support growth over time.

A resource may enter the system with limited information.

Example:

Initial entry:

- Unknown tournament photo
- Approximate year
- Source person

Later enrichment:

- Exact event identified
- Participants confirmed
- Location verified
- Historical significance documented

The system must encourage preservation first and research second.

---

## 2.3 Required Does Not Mean Complete

Not every resource requires every metadata field.

Required fields establish minimum preservation standards.

Additional fields improve understanding.

---

# 3. Metadata Categories

The Stokely Historical Preservation system uses the following metadata categories.

---

# 3.1 Identity Metadata

## Purpose

Defines what the item is.

## Core Fields

### Identifier

A unique internal reference.

Example:

SHP-RESOURCE-000001

---

### Title

The primary name assigned to the resource.

Example:

"Scott Stokely Disc Golf Instruction Seminar"

---

### Alternate Titles

Other known names or descriptions.

---

### Resource Type

The category of preserved item.

Examples:

- Photograph
- Video
- Audio Recording
- Document
- Interview
- Artifact
- Social Media Post

---

# 3.2 Descriptive Metadata

## Purpose

Explains the content and significance of the resource.

## Core Fields

### Description

A factual summary of the resource.

Should answer:

- What is this?
- Who or what does it involve?
- Why is it preserved?

---

### Subject

Topics represented by the resource.

Examples:

- Disc golf instruction
- Professional competition
- Athletic development

---

### Keywords

Searchable terms associated with the resource.

---

### Historical Significance

A description of why the item matters.

This field must distinguish:

- Known significance
- Potential significance
- Interpretation

---

# 3.3 Temporal Metadata

## Purpose

Captures historical time relationships.

## Core Fields

### Creation Date

When the resource itself was created.

---

### Event Date

When the historical event represented occurred.

---

### Date Precision

Indicates certainty.

Examples:

- Exact date
- Month and year
- Year only
- Approximate period
- Unknown

---

### Historical Era

The broader time period associated with the resource.

---

# 3.4 Spatial Metadata

## Purpose

Captures geographic relationships.

## Core Fields

### Location

The place associated with the resource.

---

### Venue

Specific location within a broader place.

Examples:

- Course
- Park
- Tournament venue

---

### Geographic Notes

Additional location context.

---

# 3.5 Entity Relationship Metadata

## Purpose

Connects resources to the historical knowledge graph.

## Core Fields

### Related People

Individuals connected to the resource.

---

### Related Organizations

Groups connected to the resource.

---

### Related Events

Historical events represented or referenced.

---

### Related Places

Locations connected to the resource.

---

### Related Resources

Other materials connected to this item.

---

# 3.6 Provenance Metadata

## Purpose

Records the origin and history of the resource.

## Core Fields

### Source

Where the resource originated.

Examples:

- Personal collection
- Interview
- Publication
- Website
- Archive

---

### Creator

Person or organization responsible for creating the resource.

---

### Acquisition Information

How the resource entered the collection.

Examples:

- Donated
- Purchased
- Licensed
- Collected
- Digitized

---

### Chain of Custody

Known ownership and transfer history.

---

### Modification History

Changes made after creation.

Examples:

- Digitization
- Restoration
- Editing
- Formatting

---

# 3.7 Evidence Metadata

## Purpose

Documents the historical reliability and research value of a resource.

## Core Fields

### Evidence Type

Examples:

- Primary source
- Secondary source
- Oral history
- Contemporary reporting
- Personal recollection

---

### Verification Status

Examples:

- Verified
- Partially verified
- Unverified
- Disputed

---

### Confidence Level

Examples:

- High
- Medium
- Low
- Unknown

---

### Supports

Identifies historical claims or interpretations supported by the resource.

---

# 3.8 Rights Metadata

## Purpose

Protects responsible use.

## Core Fields

### Rights Holder

Person or organization controlling usage rights.

---

### Copyright Status

Examples:

- Known copyright
- Public domain
- Permission required
- Unknown

---

### Usage Permissions

Defines approved uses.

Examples:

- Internal preservation
- Research
- Educational use
- Public exhibition

---

### Restrictions

Documents limitations.

---

# 4. Metadata Minimum Standard

Every preserved resource should attempt to capture:

## Required Minimum

- Identifier
- Title
- Resource Type
- Description
- Source
- Date or approximate date
- Related entities if known
- Rights status if known

---

# 5. Metadata Quality Standards

## Accuracy

Metadata should represent known information.

---

## Transparency

Unknown information should be marked unknown.

---

## Consistency

Controlled vocabulary should be used whenever possible.

---

## Traceability

Important metadata changes should be documented.

---

# 6. Metadata and Historical Claims

Metadata supports historical understanding but does not create historical truth by itself.

Example:

A photograph's metadata may identify:

- Date
- Location
- People present

However:

The historical interpretation of why that moment mattered requires evidence and analysis.

---

# 7. Metadata Evolution

Metadata standards may expand as the collection grows.

Changes should:

- Preserve compatibility
- Respect existing records
- Improve historical understanding

---

# 8. Status
